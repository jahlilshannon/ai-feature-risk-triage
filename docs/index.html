AI Feature Risk Triage

Describe a proposed AI feature. Get an evidence-tagged assessment, a recommendation with conditions attached, and the list of questions a human still has to answer.

It does not approve anything. It is not a compliance certification. It produces an argument that a person then agrees or disagrees with.

▲
Status: early. The scoring bands have not been tested against real features. The restaurant scenario is invented and illustrative. Treat every output as a starting point for a conversation, not a verdict.

The problem

Most risk questionnaires produce the same report for every feature

Boxes get ticked, a severity gets picked from a dropdown, and the output satisfies a process without telling anyone anything they did not already know. Two habits cause that.

Severity gets asserted, not derived

Someone picks "High" because the feature feels important, and nothing connects that word to what actually happens when the feature is wrong. Once a rating is in a document it tends to stay there.

The assessment inherits the claim

"No, this does not touch sensitive data" flows straight through to the conclusion, with no distinction between what was stated, what was inferred, and what nobody looked into.

This framework separates those three, and it makes severity something a team writes down in advance rather than chooses in the moment.

The questionnaire

Seventeen dimensions in five groups

The questions are domain-neutral. What the answers mean is not, which is what the profile supplies. Dimensions 9 and 16 are conditional and only appear when an earlier answer triggers them.

#DimensionWhat it establishes
Why it grew from ten dimensions to seventeen

The first draft had ten. Writing the allergen example broke four of them.

The risk driver in that example is stale menu data, and the original ten had no dimension that could have caught it. The framework would have missed, in its own showcase scenario, the exact thing the prose correctly identified. That produced dimension 10.

The same example takes free text from an anonymous diner, which is an untrusted channel into a generative model with access to a business record system, and nothing asked about that. That produced dimension 9.

The invoice scenario turns on volume and the resume scenario turns on whether the affected person can contest the outcome. Neither was representable. Those produced dimensions 5 and 8.

The remaining three are structural. Dimension 11 exists because the original questionnaire promised a likelihood rating while supplying no input that could derive one, which is precisely the failure this framework opens by criticizing. Dimension 16 exists because "data leaves the boundary" is not a risk statement until you know the contract it leaves under. Dimension 17 exists because an assessment with no named owner is a document, not a control.

Seventeen questions is a real cost, and long questionnaires get filled in badly or not at all. Two mitigations: two dimensions are conditional, and the group structure means a reviewer who reads nothing else can read Consequence and Visibility and get most of the answer.

Where this sits

Against published standards

This framework does not claim conformance with any of these. It is a triage step that runs earlier and cheaper than a formal assessment, built so its output can feed one. The questionnaire sits almost entirely inside the NIST AI RMF MAP function.

What this framework adds

Every document above requires that risk be assessed against criteria. None tells you how to establish the criteria, and in practice that is where assessments fail. The contribution here is narrow: severity meaning is written into the profile before any feature is assessed, by the team, where it can be reviewed and disagreed with later. Plus the evidence tagging, so a reader can tell which findings were asserted, which were derived, and which nobody checked. Neither is novel research. Both get skipped.

Limits

What this is not

Not a compliance certification, not a legal review, not a substitute for counsel. Not a gate, and it has no authority to approve anything.

The severity anchors are judgment written in advance by a team, so a profile written by someone with no incident history is a guess in a nicer format. The likelihood inputs are weak before a system exists, because evaluation coverage is usually unknown pre-build. And this covers pre-build triage only: no post-market monitoring, no incident response, no ongoing measurement.

If it ever starts functioning as a rubber stamp, it has failed at the thing it was built for.
